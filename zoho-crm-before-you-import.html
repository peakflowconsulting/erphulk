Five things to set up in Zoho CRM before you import a single lead

Most messy CRMs were messy on day one. Fix these five settings first and your data stays clean for years.

Here's a pattern we see every few weeks. A business buys Zoho CRM, someone exports 4,000 contacts from an old system or a spreadsheet, hits import on day one, and starts selling. Six months later nobody trusts the reports, there are three records for the same customer, and the sales head is back to tracking deals in Excel.

The CRM wasn't the problem. The first hour was. Before you import anything, sort out these five things.

1. Decide your stages before you decide your fields

Write down the steps a deal actually goes through in your business. Not the textbook version, yours. A distributor might go Enquiry, Quote sent, Sample sent, Negotiation, Won, Lost. A software reseller looks completely different.

Keep it to six or seven stages. Each one should be something a salesperson can say yes or no to without arguing. “Interested” is not a stage. “Quote sent” is.

2. Set up duplicate checks

Mark email and phone as unique fields on Leads and Contacts. It takes two minutes in the field settings and it stops the same customer turning up three times with slightly different spellings.

Do this before the import, because the import wizard can then skip or merge duplicates for you. Doing it after means cleaning up by hand.

3. Agree on lead sources, and make the field mandatory

If you ever want to know whether LinkedIn, your website or trade shows bring in the good customers, you need a lead source on every record. Make it a picklist with a short, fixed list. No free text, and no “Other” unless you really need it.

The question every owner asks after six months is “where are our best customers coming from?” This field is the only way to answer it.

4. Sort out who sees what

Roles and profiles feel like an IT job, so people skip them. Then a new hire can see every deal in the company, or a manager can't see their own team's pipeline.

A simple setup works for most small teams: sales reps see their own records, managers see their team's, and the owner sees everything. Set the roles first, then invite users.

5. Clean the file before it goes in

Open the spreadsheet and fix the boring stuff: one format for phone numbers, country names spelled the same way, company names without “Pvt Ltd” in five different styles. Delete rows with no email and no phone. You won't call them anyway.

Then import a test batch of 50 rows. Check them, fix the mapping, and only then bring in the rest.

The short version

  • Stages you can say yes or no to
  • Unique email and phone
  • A mandatory lead source picklist
  • Roles before users
  • A clean file, and a 50-row test first

None of this is clever. It's just the part everyone wants to skip, and it's the difference between a CRM people use and one they work around.
