Zoho One or separate apps? How a 30-person company should decide

Zoho One looks like the obvious deal. Sometimes it is. Here is the simple test we run with clients before they sign.

Zoho One gives you more than 45 apps under one licence, and on paper it nearly always looks like the better deal. We still talk some clients out of it. Here's how we decide.

Count the apps you'll actually use in the first year

Not the ones you might use someday. The ones that will have real data and real users in the next twelve months. For most small businesses that's somewhere between three and six: CRM, Books, maybe Inventory, People or Payroll, and a forms or desk tool.

If your list is two apps, buy the two apps. If it's four or more, Zoho One starts to make sense, and the more apps on the list, the stronger the case.

Check how many people need access

Zoho One is priced per employee, and on most plans that means everyone on the payroll, not just the people who log in. A 30-person company where only 8 people touch the systems can end up paying for 22 seats it doesn't use.

So do the maths both ways with the current price list in front of you. It's ten minutes on a spreadsheet and it often settles the question.

Think about who will run it

More apps means more settings, more users to manage and more things that can be configured badly. If nobody in the company owns the setup, a big suite turns into a big mess faster.

The licence is the cheap part. The setup and the habits around it are where the money goes.

Our rule of thumb

  • One to three apps, small team: buy them separately.
  • Four or more apps, most staff using at least one: Zoho One is usually worth it.
  • Somewhere in between: start with the separate apps and move to Zoho One when the third or fourth app arrives. Zoho makes that switch fairly painless.

Prices and plan rules change, so check the current numbers on Zoho's own pricing page before you sign anything.
