This week in ERP: Odoo patches a serious bug, NetSuite sells speed, and Zoho Books checks into hotels

Five ERP stories from the last few weeks, and what each one means if you run a business of 10 to 200 people.

Quick note before we start: this is the first ERP Hulk roundup. Most weeks will cover the last seven days. This one reaches back a few weeks to catch up. Every item links to where we read it.

1. Odoo patches a SQL injection bug. If you self-host 16.0 or older, act this week

Odoo published security advisory ODOO-SA-2026-11-23 on 23 November. It covers two problems: a SQL injection flaw (CVE-2026-130028) that hits Odoo 16.0 and older, and a cross-site scripting flaw (CVE-2026-130029) that reaches all the way up to 20.0.

What it means for you: if you're on Odoo Online, Odoo handles the patching. If your Odoo runs on your own server, or a partner hosts it for you, ask them today whether the fix is in. Plenty of small businesses are still on 16.0 or older because “it works”. This is the kind of week that makes that risky.

2. NetSuite says it can now go live in weeks, not months

At SuiteWorld, Oracle NetSuite launched SuiteExpress. It uses AI to pull your meeting notes, spreadsheets and old system data into a shared knowledge base, then uses that to speed up the implementation. NetSuite claims it cuts rollout time “to weeks, or even days”. For now it is only for software companies in North America, with other industries and regions planned for 2027.

What it means for you: not much yet, unless you're a North American software firm. But watch it. Implementation time is the biggest hidden cost in any ERP project, and every vendor will now be pushed to promise shorter rollouts. When they do, ask what they're assuming about the state of your data.

3. Zoho Books gets a hotel connection

Hotelogix, a hotel property management system, now connects to Zoho Books. Bookings, payments and invoices from the front desk flow into the accounts without someone re-typing them. It's aimed at independent and mid-sized hotels.

What it means for you: if you run a hotel on Hotelogix and do your books in Excel or Tally, this is a good moment to look at Zoho Books. The bigger point is the one we keep making: the ERP matters less than how well it connects to the system your team already lives in.

4. Business Central picks up an easier EDI connector

Business Central users can now connect to Orderful's network of over 10,000 EDI trading partners through a connector from TMC Global, listed on Microsoft Marketplace.

What it means for you: if you supply big retailers who insist on EDI, setting that up on Business Central just got less painful. EDI has quietly stopped plenty of distributors from moving off older systems.

5. NetSuite consultancies keep changing hands

Boston firm WilliamsMarston bought the NetSuite services team from Charted, an accounts payable software company. Charted keeps its software business and the two will work together on shared clients.

What it means for you: if your ERP partner gets bought, your project usually carries on, but the people and prices can change. Check who your named consultant is after any deal, and get your support terms in writing.

The one to act on

Number one. Everything else on this list can wait until Monday. An unpatched ERP holding your customers, invoices and bank details can't.
