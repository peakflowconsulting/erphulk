Zoho One or just Books and Inventory? How a 30-person company should decide

Zoho One looks like the obvious deal. Sometimes it is. Here is the simple test we run with clients before they sign.

Zoho One gives you more than 45 apps under one licence, and on paper it nearly always looks like the better deal. We still talk some clients out of it and into just Zoho Books and Zoho Inventory. Here's how we decide.

Count the apps you'll actually use in the first year

Not the ones you might use someday. The ones that will have real data and real users in the next twelve months. For a trading or distribution business that's usually Books and Inventory, sometimes Payroll and People, and maybe one or two more.

If your list is Books and Inventory, buy Books and Inventory. If it's four or more apps, Zoho One starts to make sense, and the longer the list, the stronger the case.

Check how many people need access

Zoho One is priced per employee, and on most plans that means everyone on the payroll, not just the people who log in. A 30-person company where only 8 people touch the finance and stock systems can end up paying for 22 seats it doesn't use.

So do the maths both ways with the current price list in front of you. It's ten minutes on a spreadsheet and it often settles the question.

Think about who will run it

More apps means more settings, more users and more things that can be set up badly. If nobody in the company owns the system, a big suite turns into a big mess faster.

The licence is the cheap part. The setup and the habits around it are where the money goes.

Our rule of thumb

  • Books and Inventory only, small team: buy them separately.
  • Four or more apps, most staff using at least one: Zoho One is usually worth it.
  • Somewhere in between: start with the separate apps and move to Zoho One when the third or fourth app arrives. Zoho makes that switch fairly painless.

Prices and plan rules change, so check the current numbers on Zoho's own pricing page before you sign anything.
